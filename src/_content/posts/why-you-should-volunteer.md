Every year, DjangoCon US comes together because a group of people shows up early, stays late, and does the behind-the-scenes work that keeps everything running. Things like printing badges, timing rooms, setting up speaker support, and making sure speakers feel welcomed right before they step on stage don’t just happen automatically, volunteers make them happen.

This year, DjangoCon US is in Chicago from **August 24–28, 2026, at the voco Chicago Downtown.** And this year, we’re trying something a little different: instead of splitting the event into multiple tracks, we’ll be running a single track throughout the conference. That means one room, one talk at a time, and everyone is part of the same shared experience.

It’s a small change in how the schedule is structured, but it makes a big difference. Volunteer shifts won’t just impact one “section” of the community; you'll be supporting the entire event and everyone taking part in it.


## The Job Nobody Sees (And Why It Still Matters)

Here’s the truth about volunteering at a conference: the best volunteer roles are often invisible.

A first-time attendee finds the right room quickly because someone helps them. A speaker who’s a little nervous gets reassurance from the timer and can focus on the talk. A small act like keeping coffee stocked or guiding someone to the right place helps the conference feel welcoming instead of confusing.

No one starts a blog post about the person who quietly made sure things worked. But ask any experienced attendee what makes a conference feel warm, and they’ll tell you: it’s the people quietly holding everything together.

That’s what volunteering lets you do. And honestly? It’s also one of the fastest ways to feel like you truly belong.


## First-Time Volunteers: We’ve Got You

If this is your first time volunteering, don’t worry, Rachell and Monica will be there to help you get started. You won’t be handed a clipboard and expected to figure everything out on your own.

Each shift comes with guidance, an overview of what to expect, and someone you can check in with if anything is unclear. First-time volunteers aren’t an afterthought here—they’re planned for and supported.

You don’t need a lot of experience to be helpful. If you can greet people, point them in the right direction, or help keep a talk running on time, you already have what it takes. The rest is covered.

![Person smiling at a table during an event.](/assets/img/blog/monica-oyugi.jpg)


## Ready to Join?

If you’ve been thinking about volunteering but haven’t signed up yet, consider this your nudge. You don’t need prior experience, and you don’t need to know everyone, you just need a few hours and the willingness to show up.

[Ready to volunteer?](https://automation.defna.org/volunteers/) We’ll see you in Chicago!
